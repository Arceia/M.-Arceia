MIRU & MILO

The Bell of Forgotten Names

by M. Arceia

Prologue

The Door at the End of the Field

There are stories about places that do not exist. Most people hear those stories once, laugh about them, and continue with their lives.

Miru asks for directions.

That was how she ended up beyond the last farm, halfway through a field of grass tall enough to slap her in the face, following instructions from an elderly shepherd who had ended his tale with the words, “But do not actually go there.”

A stalk caught Miru across the nose. She pushed it aside. “People should stop adding that part if they want me to listen.”

The grass rustled around her. It sounded judgmental.

The rumor was simple. Somewhere beyond the eastern hill stood a door with no house around it. It appeared only to people who were lost, curious, foolish, or some unfortunate combination of the three.

Miru qualified twice. Possibly three times, depending on how far she had wandered from the road.

She reached the top of the hill and stopped.

The door stood in the middle of the field. No walls. No roof. No fence. Just an old wooden door inside a freestanding stone frame, as if a house had vanished and forgotten to take its entrance.

Miru approached it slowly. Not cautiously. Slowly. There was a difference. Cautious people planned for danger. Miru was trying to look dramatic in case somebody was watching.

She walked around the frame. The field continued behind it. She walked around it again. Still a field. On the third circle, she crouched and inspected the ground beneath the door. Grass grew through the gap at the bottom. A beetle crawled beneath it, entirely unimpressed by the impossible architecture.

Miru stood. “Well, you are not very helpful.”

The beetle continued with its day.

She knocked. Three polite taps.

The sound echoed much farther than wood should have allowed. Miru leaned closer. “Hello?”

Nothing.

She knocked again. “Mysterious door inspection.”

A cloud crossed the sun. A cricket chirped. The door remained professionally mysterious.

Miru looked for a keyhole. There was none.

“Inconvenient.”

The brass handle gleamed.

The sensible decision would have been to return to the shepherd and report what she had found. The slightly less sensible decision would have been to mark the location, gather supplies, and come back with help.

Miru wrapped both hands around the handle.

“I am only checking whether it is locked.”

The door opened.

The field disappeared.

No thunder. No explosion. No helpful warning from the sky. One moment there was grass and sunlight. The next, there was an aisle stretching farther than sight.

Miru remained in the doorway.

Shelves rose like towers on both sides of her. Ladders climbed into golden mist. Bridges crossed impossible distances overhead. Books filled every space, from tiny volumes no larger than her thumb to iron-bound things that looked capable of eating lesser books. Pages whispered to one another.

The air smelled of paper, dust, rain, smoke, old ink, and the moment before a story begins. Miru leaned inside. The nearest lamp brightened.

She leaned back. It dimmed.

Miru leaned in again.

Bright.

Back.

Dim.

She did this four more times.

Somewhere in the Library, a book slammed shut with the weariness of an employee watching a customer play with the automatic doors.

Miru stepped through.

The door swung closed behind her.

She turned.

The field was gone. The door was gone. There was only another shelf, packed from floor to ceiling with books that had not been there a second ago.

Miru stared at the empty air. Then she turned back toward the Impossible Library.

“Well.”
